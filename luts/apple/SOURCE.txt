Apple Log conversion LUTs — provenance
======================================

Files
-----
  AppleLogToRec709-v1.0.cube   65x65x65 3D LUT. Apple Log -> Rec.709 (display).
  AppleLogToLin-v1.0.cube      4096-entry 1D LUT. Apple Log -> scene-linear.
  AppleLogLUTsv1.0.zip         The original download, kept unmodified.

Where they came from
--------------------
Downloaded from Apple's developer downloads page on 11 Sep 2026:

  https://developer.apple.com/download/all/?q=Apple%20log%20profile

Search that page for "Apple Log Profile Conversion LUT". These are Apple's own
official conversion LUTs, not a third-party emulation.

Access: a FREE Apple ID is enough. Sign in at developer.apple.com and accept the
(free) Apple Developer Agreement once; you do NOT need the paid $99/year Apple
Developer Program — that is only for publishing to the App Store. The files are
not indexed by the developer site's own search, so the URL above is the way in.

Verified on download
--------------------
  AppleLogToRec709-v1.0.cube   TITLE "Apple Log to Rec709 3DLUT - v1.0", LUT_3D_SIZE 65
  AppleLogToLin-v1.0.cube      TITLE "Apple Log to linear LUT v1.0",     LUT_1D_SIZE 4096
                               output range -0.0564 .. 12.0000

Why these and not a third-party LUT
-----------------------------------
Apple Log's transfer function is proprietary and ffmpeg has no built-in support
for it, so the conversion cannot be approximated with a curve — it needs Apple's
own table. Measured against standardised colours in frame (see docs/PIPELINE.md),
the Rec709 LUT is colorimetrically accurate out of the box: it lands the RAL 5017
traffic-blue sign at B/G 1.99 against a 1.98 spec with no correction applied.
Every hand-rolled alternative tried during development measured worse.

The LogToLin LUT is what makes the scene-linear route possible (log -> linear ->
filmic tone map -> Rec.709). That route is implemented in
scripts/make-filmic-lut.py but is NOT what ships — see docs/PIPELINE.md,
"Approach A", for why it lost to the simpler path.
